🩺 Chapter 4: Human Health

Understand the differences between infectious and non-infectious diseases, and how our body's immune system fights them.

🤒 4.1 Infectious & Non-Infectious Diseases

Diseases can be classified into two main categories:

  • Infectious Diseases: Caused by pathogens (viruses, bacteria, fungi) and can be transmitted from one person to another.
    - Examples: Tuberculosis (TB), Dengue fever, Cholera, COVID-19.
  • Non-Infectious Diseases: Caused by genetics or lifestyle choices and cannot be transmitted to others.
    - Examples: Cancer, Diabetes, Asthma, Cardiovascular diseases.

🦠 4.2 How Are Diseases Transmitted?

Pathogens spread through several mediums and vectors:

  • Airborne: Through droplet infection (sneezing, coughing) or dust. (e.g., Flu, TB).
  • Waterborne: Consuming water contaminated with faeces/pathogens (e.g., Cholera).
  • Direct Contact: Touching infected persons or sexual contact (e.g., Ringworm, Syphilis).
  • Vectors: Animals that transmit pathogens.
    - Aedes mosquito: Dengue virus.
    - Anopheles mosquito: Malaria parasite.
    - Housefly/Rat: Salmonella, Leptospirosis.

🛡️ 4.3 Body Defence System

Our body has a 3-line defence mechanism to fight against pathogens.

  • First Line of Defence (External): Prevents pathogens from entering.
    - Skin: Tough physical barrier. Secretes sebum and sweat that kill bacteria.
    - Mucous Membrane & Lysozyme: Mucus traps dust/bacteria. Lysozyme (in tears, saliva) destroys bacteria.
  • Second Line of Defence (Internal): White blood cells engulf and digest pathogens through a process called Phagocytosis.
  • Third Line of Defence (Immune System): White blood cells produce antibodies to destroy specific pathogens (antigens).

💉 4.4 Types of Immunity

Immunity is the body's ability to resist infections. It is divided into Active and Passive immunity.

  • Active Immunity: Body produces its own antibodies. Lasts for a long time.
    - Natural Active: Recovering from a disease (e.g., Chickenpox).
    - Artificial Active: Receiving a vaccine (dead/weakened pathogens).
  • Passive Immunity: Body receives ready-made antibodies. Fast but temporary.
    - Natural Passive: Antibodies passed from mother to baby (placenta/breast milk).
    - Artificial Passive: Injection of antiserum for immediate cure (e.g., snake antivenom).

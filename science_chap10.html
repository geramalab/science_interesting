🔊 Chapter 10: Sound Waves

Discover how sound is produced, how waves travel through different mediums, characteristics of sound, and reflection of sound.

🎵 10.1 Characteristics of Sound Waves

Sound is a form of energy produced by vibrations and travels as longitudinal waves.

  • Production of Sound: Produced by vibrating objects (e.g., guitar strings, vocal cords, beating drums).
  • Medium Requirement: Sound requires a medium (solid, liquid, or gas) to travel. It cannot travel through a vacuum (empty space).
  • Speed of Sound: Travels fastest in solids, slower in liquids, and slowest in gases.

📊 10.2 Pitch and Loudness of Sound

How we perceive different sound properties based on wave patterns.

  • Pitch: Depends on the frequency (measured in Hertz, Hz).
    - High frequency = High pitch (e.g., whistle, bird chirp).
    - Low frequency = Low pitch (e.g., roar of a lion, bass guitar).
  • Loudness: Depends on the amplitude (measured in decibels, dB).
    - Large amplitude = Loud sound.
    - Small amplitude = Soft/quiet sound.

🗣️ 10.3 Reflection of Sound and Echo

Sound waves bounce off hard surfaces when they hit obstacles.

  • Echo: The reflection of sound waves from a hard surface back to the listener.
  • Applications of Reflection:
    - SONAR: Used by ships to measure ocean depth and detect underwater objects.
    - Ultrasound Scan: Used in medicine to check the development of a fetus.
    - Echolocation: Used by bats and dolphins to navigate and hunt.

⚠️ 10.4 Phenomena and Limitation of Hearing

Understanding human ear sensitivity and noise pollution.

  • Auditory Range: Humans can normally hear sounds with frequencies between 20 Hz and 20,000 Hz.
  • Ultrasound & Infrasound:
    - Ultrasound: Frequencies above 20,000 Hz.
    - Infrasound: Frequencies below 20 Hz.
  • Noise Pollution: Excessive loud noises can cause hearing impairment, stress, and high blood pressure. Soundproofing materials help reduce echo and noise.

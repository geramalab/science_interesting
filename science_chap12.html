🪐 Chapter 12: The Solar System

Discover the structure of the Solar System, planet characteristics, gravitational forces, and relationships between celestial bodies.

☀️ 12.1 The Solar System

The Solar System consists of the Sun, planets, dwarf planets, natural satellites, asteroids, and comets orbiting the Sun.

  • The Sun: The center of the Solar System and the main source of gravitational pull and energy.
  • Order of Planets (from the Sun): Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. (Mnemonic: My Very Energetic Mother Just Served Us Noodles).

🪐 12.2 Planetary Characteristics

Planets differ in size, composition, distance from the Sun, temperature, and moons.

  • Inner Planets (Terrestrial Planets): Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars. Smaller in size, made mostly of rock and metal.
  • Outer Planets (Gas Giants): Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Much larger, made mostly of hydrogen and helium gases.
  • Unique Features: Venus is the hottest planet due to a runaway greenhouse effect; Saturn has prominent ice rings.

🌍 12.3 Gravitational Force and Weight

Gravity keeps planets in orbit around the Sun and moons in orbit around planets.

  • Gravity: A pulling force between any two objects with mass. The larger the mass of the Sun, the stronger its gravitational pull on the planets.
  • Weight vs Mass: Weight depends on gravitational field strength (W = mg), whereas mass is the amount of matter in an object and remains constant everywhere.

🌙 12.4 Interrelationships and Conditions for Life

Earth has precise conditions that support life compared to other planets.

  • Conditions for Life on Earth: Presence of liquid water, breathable oxygen atmosphere, suitable temperature range, and protection from harmful solar radiation by Earth's magnetic field.

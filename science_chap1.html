🌍 Chapter 1: Biodiversity

Learn about the incredible variety of living organisms on Earth and how we classify them.

🌱 1.1 What is Biodiversity?

Biodiversity refers to the diversity of living organisms (plants, animals, microorganisms) interacting within an ecosystem.

  • Factors affecting biodiversity: Habitat, climate (temperature, rainfall).
  • Endemic Species: Species that are found exclusively in one specific location. Example: Malayan Tiger, Rafflesia, Hornbill.
  • Importance: Balance in nature, sources of food, medical research, and raw materials for industries.

🦁 1.2 Classification of Animals

Animals are divided into two main groups based on the presence of a backbone.

  • Invertebrates: Animals without a backbone.
    - Without legs: Earthworm (without segmented body), Tapeworm.
    - With legs: Insects (3 pairs of legs), Spiders/Prawns (more than 3 pairs).
  • Vertebrates: Animals with a backbone.
    - FISH: Cold-blooded, breathe through gills, lay eggs.
    - AMPHIBIANS: Cold-blooded, live on land and in water (frog).
    - REPTILES: Cold-blooded, scaly skin (snake, crocodile).
    - BIRDS: Warm-blooded, feathers, wings (penguin, eagle).
    - MAMMALS: Warm-blooded, give birth, produce milk (human, whale, bat).

🌿 1.3 Classification of Plants

Plants are generally classified into non-flowering and flowering plants.

  • Non-Flowering Plants: Do not produce flowers, seeds, or fruits. Reproduce via spores or cones.
    - Examples: Moss (lumut), Fern (paku pakis), Conifer (pokok konifer).
  • Flowering Plants: Produce flowers that become fruits containing seeds. Divided into two types:
    - Monocotyledon: One seed leaf, fibrous root, leaves with parallel veins (e.g., paddy, maize, grass).
    - Dicotyledon: Two seed leaves, tap root, leaves with network veins (e.g., durian tree, rubber tree).

🔑 1.4 Dichotomous Key

A scientific method used by biologists to identify and classify organisms.

  • It consists of a series of choices that lead the user to the correct name of a given item.
  • How it works: Always starts with general characteristics and moves to more specific ones, dividing organisms into two distinct groups at each step.
  • Example: "Does it have a backbone?" (Yes -> Vertebrate / No -> Invertebrate).

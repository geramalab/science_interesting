☄️ Chapter 13: Meteoroid, Asteroid and Comet

Learn about minor celestial bodies in the Solar System, their characteristics, and their impacts on Earth.

🪨 13.1 Asteroid

Large rocky and metallic bodies orbiting the Sun.

  • Composition: Made up of rocks and metals (iron and nickel).
  • Location: Most asteroids are found in the Asteroid Belt located between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter.
  • Size: Ranges from a few metres to hundreds of kilometres wide (e.g., Ceres).

🌠 13.2 Meteoroid, Meteor, and Meteorite

Small rocky or metallic fragments moving through space.

  • Meteoroid: Small debris floating in space originating from asteroids or comets.
  • Meteor (Shooting Star): A meteoroid that enters Earth's atmosphere and burns up due to friction, producing a streak of light.
  • Meteorite: A meteoroid that is large enough not to burn up completely and successfully hits Earth's surface, creating impact craters.

✨ 13.3 Comet

Icy celestial bodies that travel in long, elliptical orbits around the Sun.

  • Composition: Made of frozen gases, water ice, rocks, and dust ("dirty snowballs").
  • Structure: Consists of a solid nucleus and a glowing coma with a long tail that always points away from the Sun due to solar wind.
  • Origin: Mostly originate from the Kuiper Belt and Oort Cloud at the edge of the Solar System.

💥 13.4 Potential Impact on Earth

Understanding the risks and historical impacts of near-Earth objects (NEOs).

  • Meteorite Craters: Past impacts have formed massive craters on Earth (e.g., meteorite impacts linked to dinosaur extinction).
  • Planetary Defense: Scientists continuously monitor near-Earth asteroids and comets to calculate collision risks and develop deflection technologies.

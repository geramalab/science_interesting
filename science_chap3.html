🍎 Chapter 3: Nutrition

Discover the 7 classes of food, the importance of a balanced diet, and the fascinating journey of food through our digestive system.

🍞 3.1 Classes of Food

Food is divided into seven main classes based on their functions in the human body:

  • Carbohydrates: Main source of energy (e.g., rice, bread, potatoes).
  • Proteins: Essential for growth and repairing damaged tissues (e.g., meat, fish, eggs, milk).
  • Fats: Provides 2x more energy than carbs and acts as a heat insulator (e.g., butter, cooking oil).
  • Vitamins & Minerals: Protects the body from diseases and maintains health (e.g., fruits, vegetables, salt, seafood).
  • Fibre: Prevents constipation by stimulating peristalsis (e.g., cereals, vegetables).
  • Water: Regulates body temperature and transports nutrients.

⚖️ 3.2 Importance of a Balanced Diet

A balanced diet contains all the food classes in the right quantities and proportions according to individual needs.

  • Factors Affecting Calorific Requirement:
    - Age: Growing teenagers need more energy than adults.
    - Gender: Males generally require more energy than females of the same age.
    - Physical Activity: A construction worker needs more energy than a clerk.
    - Body Size: Larger individuals require more calories.
    - State of Health: Sick people need suitable diets to recover.
  • The energy value of food is measured in Joules (J) or Calories (cal).

👄 3.3 Human Digestive System

Digestion is the process of breaking down large and complex pieces of food into smaller, simpler, and soluble molecules.

  • Physical Digestion: Chewing in the mouth and churning in the stomach.
  • Chemical Digestion: Uses enzymes to break down food.
    - Mouth: Salivary amylase breaks down starch into maltose.
    - Oesophagus: Pushes food down via peristalsis.
    - Stomach: Gastric juice contains protease (digests protein) and hydrochloric acid (kills bacteria).
    - Duodenum: Receives bile (emulsifies fat) and pancreatic juice (amylase, protease, lipase).

🩸 3.4 Absorption and Defecation

After digestion, nutrients must be absorbed into the bloodstream.

  • Small Intestine: The inner wall is covered with millions of tiny, finger-like projections called villi.
    - Villi increase the surface area for maximum nutrient absorption.
  • Large Intestine: Undigested food (faeces) enters here. The main function is to reabsorb water and minerals.
  • Rectum & Anus: Faeces are temporarily stored in the rectum and eliminated through the anus (a process called defecation).

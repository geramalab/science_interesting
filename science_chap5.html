💧 Chapter 5: Water and Solution

Understand the physical characteristics of water, how solutions are formed, and methods of water purification.

🌊 5.1 Physical Characteristics of Water

Water is a chemical compound consisting of hydrogen and oxygen ($H_2O$).

  • State of Matter: Exists as liquid (water), solid (ice), and gas (steam/water vapour).
  • Boiling & Melting Point: Pure water boils at $100^\circ\text{C}$ and freezes/melts at $0^\circ\text{C}$.
  • Density: Pure water has a density of $1 \text{ g/cm}^3$ at $4^\circ\text{C}$. Ice is less dense than liquid water, which is why ice floats.
  • Surface Tension: Caused by the attraction between water molecules at the surface (allows small insects to walk on water).
  • Capillary Action: The ability of water to move up narrow tubes against gravity (vital for plants).

🧪 5.2 Solutions and Solubility

Mixing substances creates mixtures known as solutions, suspension, or colloids.

  • Solute: The substance that dissolves (e.g., salt, sugar).
  • Solvent: The liquid that dissolves the solute (e.g., water, alcohol).
  • Solution: Formed when a solute dissolves in a solvent (e.g., saltwater).
  • Dilute vs Concentrated vs Saturated:
    - Dilute Solution: Contains a small amount of solute.
    - Concentrated Solution: Contains a large amount of solute.
    - Saturated Solution: Contains the maximum amount of solute that can dissolve at a specific temperature.

🌡️ 5.3 Rate of Solubility

How fast a solute dissolves in a solvent depends on several factors:

  • Temperature of Solvent: Higher temperature increases the rate of dissolving (sugar dissolves faster in hot water).
  • Rate of Stirring: Stirring speeds up the dissolving process by bringing fresh solvent into contact with the solute.
  • Particle Size of Solute: Smaller particles (powdered sugar) dissolve faster than larger chunks (sugar cubes) because of greater surface area.

🚰 5.4 Water Purification System

Natural water sources contain impurities and must be treated before consumption.

  • Methods of Purification:
    - Filtration: Removes suspended solid particles.
    - Boiling: Kills microorganisms and pathogens.
    - Distillation: Separates pure water from dissolved substances through evaporation and condensation.
    - Chlorination/Fluoridation: Added to public water supplies to kill bacteria and prevent tooth decay.

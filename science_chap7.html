⚡ Chapter 7: Energy and Power

Master the concepts of work, energy, potential and kinetic energy, conservation of energy, and power calculations.

🏋️‍♂️ 7.1 Work and Energy

Understanding how force, displacement, and energy relate in physics.

  • Work Done (W): The product of force applied and the displacement of an object in the direction of the force.
    - Formula: W = F × s (where F = force in Newtons, s = displacement in metres).
    - Unit: Joule (J) or Newton-metre (Nm).
  • Energy: The capacity of a body to do work. Also measured in Joules (J).

🔋 7.2 Forms and Sources of Energy

Energy exists in various forms and can be transformed from one form to another.

  • Kinetic Energy (Ek): Energy possessed by a moving object.
    - Formula: Ek = ½ × m × v² (where m = mass, v = velocity).
  • Gravitational Potential Energy (Ep): Energy stored due to an object's position above the ground.
    - Formula: Ep = m × g × h (where m = mass, g = gravitational acceleration, h = height).
  • Other Forms: Chemical, electrical, light, sound, nuclear, and thermal energy.

🔄 7.3 Principle of Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed, but it can be converted from one form to another.

  • Energy Conversion Example (Falling Object):
    - At maximum height: Potential energy is maximum, kinetic energy is zero.
    - As it falls: Potential energy converts into kinetic energy.
    - Just before hitting the ground: Kinetic energy is maximum.
  • Total energy in a closed system remains constant.

⚙️ 7.4 Power

Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.

  • Formula:
    Power (P) = Work Done (W) / Time Taken (t)
  • Unit: Watt (W) or Joules per second (J/s).
  • A higher power rating means more work is done in a shorter period of time.
